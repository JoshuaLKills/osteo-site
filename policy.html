3D-printed vs cast iron: where each one actually wins.

We sell the printed stuff, so take this with the grain of salt that implies — but we'd rather you buy the right equipment than buy from us specifically. Here's where topology-optimized printed parts genuinely beat cast iron, and where cast iron still wins outright.

Factor3D-Printed (OsTeO)Cast Iron
Weight-to-strength Wins. Lattice infill puts material only where load paths need it. A printed J-cup insert weighs a third of an equivalent solid part at the same rated load. Solid and heavy by default — the strength is real but so is the mass you didn't need.
Custom fit Wins. Made-to-order bore diameters, rack widths, and sleeve fits. No adapters. Fixed sizes. You adapt to the part, not the other way around.
Impact resistance at extreme force Good, but has a failure ceiling — a CF-nylon shell can crack under a hard enough single impact. Wins. Cast iron deforms before it shatters, and shrugs off drops that would crack a printed shell.
Repairability Wins. Crack a part, print another one from the same file. No shipping wait on a re-cast. A cracked casting is scrap. You're buying a full replacement either way.
Cost at scale Higher per-unit cost for small batches; each part is individually printed. Wins. Mass casting is cheaper once tooling is paid off, especially for simple shapes like plates.
Lead time on a new design Wins. A new design goes from CAD to a tested part in days, not months. New molds are expensive and slow. Iteration is rare once tooling exists.

Where we'd actually tell you to buy cast iron

Full-size Olympic plates and anything that gets dropped from height, repeatedly, at your max load. Cast iron's failure mode there — bending or chipping — is safer and cheaper than ours. We don't make plates for that reason.

Where printed actually makes sense

Accessories, connectors, handles, and anything that benefits from an exact fit to equipment you already own: J-cup liners, collar sets, grip sleeves, band anchors. This is the category we build for, and it's the category where the lattice-infill logic pays off without the failure-mode risk of a full barbell plate.
