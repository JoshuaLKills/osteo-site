The skeleton principle

Your femur and our infill solve the same problem.

Trabecular bone?The spongy, lattice-like bone found inside joints and at the ends of long bones — as opposed to the dense, solid "cortical" bone that forms the outer shell. is nature's answer to carrying maximum load with minimum mass. Gyroid infill?A repeating 3D curved lattice pattern used inside 3D-printed parts. It fills space efficiently in every direction, which is why it shows up in both bone and print infill. is ours. We didn't borrow the name OsTeO for branding — the manufacturing logic is genuinely the same one.

// Trabecular bone

Nature's load path

Spongy bone forms a self-similar mesh that thickens exactly along stress lines, leaving the interior porous where load doesn't travel. It's densest at the epiphysis?The rounded end of a long bone, where it meets a joint. It's built denser and more reinforced than the shaft, since that's where impact and rotational load concentrate. — the joint end — and hollow through the shaft. It's why a bird's wing bone survives flight loads at a fraction of the mass of solid ivory.

Our print path

We run finite-element analysis on every part, then let the slicer place a gyroid, cubic, or honeycomb lattice only where the stress map says it's needed. A kettlebell shell and a femoral neck are, structurally, solving the same equation.

Process

From scan to structure.

Five steps between a design file and a piece of equipment sitting in your garage.

01 02 03 04 05
01

Load-case modeling

We define how a part actually gets loaded in use — a kettlebell swing isn't a static weight, it's a rotating, decelerating one — and build the stress map from that.

02

Topology optimization?Software that removes material from low-stress regions of a design automatically, based on where the load actually travels — the digital version of what bone remodeling does naturally.

Software strips material from every region the stress map marks as low-load, leaving a bone-like skeleton of the original solid shape.

03

Lattice infill

The remaining shell gets a gyroid, cubic, or honeycomb infill tuned per-region: denser near joints and fasteners, sparser through the core.

04

Reinforced-filament printing

Carbon-fiber PETG or nylon, printed on climate-controlled beds, at 0.2mm layers for load-bearing shells and 0.12mm for grip surfaces.

05

Load testing to failure

One unit from every batch gets loaded past its rated capacity on our test rig until it fails, so the number on the label is real, not theoretical.

Why OsTeO exists

Started in a garage, still built like one.

OsTeO began with a cracked plastic J-cup and a 3D printer that was, at the time, mostly used for miniatures. The replacement part printed that night held rack-ins better than the one that broke. That felt worth pursuing further than a hobby.

Every part we sell has been load-tested past its rating on our own rig before it ships. We are not trying to out-cheap cast iron. We are trying to make equipment where the material only exists where the load actually needs it, the way a skeleton does.

Questions

Before you order.

How long does a print take to ship?+

Most parts print within 2–14 hours depending on size, and we batch-ship twice a week. Expect 3–7 business days from order to your door for in-stock geometries; custom-fit parts (like collars sized to your rack) add 1–2 days for the design pass.

What's the warranty?+

Three years on structural failure under rated load. If a part cracks along the lattice at or below its rating, we replace it free. Cosmetic wear, misuse well past the rating, or heat damage from a garage that hits 60°C in summer aren't covered.

Can you match a part I already have?+

Usually, yes. Send us the dimensions or the broken part itself and we'll quote a custom design. This is exactly how the J-cup insert and the collar set started.

Is the material safe for a home gym with kids or pets around?+

CF-Nylon and PETG-CF are both inert once printed — no off-gassing, no soft plasticizers to chew off. We wouldn't put anything in a J-cup or a handle that we wouldn't want a dog getting curious about.

What if I want a return?+

30 days on unused, uncustomized parts. Made-to-order items (custom bore diameters, non-standard collar widths) are final sale, since they're printed specifically for your equipment.
